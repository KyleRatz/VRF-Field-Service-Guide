Quick symptom guide

Low chilled-water ΔT

Verify actual flow first. Excessive flow, low building load, bypassing, three-way valves, fouled coils, or controls can all produce low ΔT. Do not condemn the chiller from ΔT alone.

High evaporator approach

Compare leaving chilled-water temperature to evaporating saturation temperature. A worsening approach can point toward poor evaporator heat transfer, low refrigerant feed, fouling, oil logging, or flow problems depending on machine design.

High condenser approach

On a water-cooled machine, first verify condenser-water flow and tower performance. Fouled tubes, scale, non-condensables, overcharge, or poor heat rejection can elevate condensing temperature.

High head on air-cooled chiller

Check condenser coil cleanliness, fan operation/direction, airflow recirculation, ambient conditions, fan staging/VFD control, charge, and non-condensables.

Low suction / freeze protection

Verify evaporator water flow before refrigerant adjustments. Check pump operation, strainers, valves, flow proving, load, leaving-water setpoint, evaporator fouling, refrigerant feed, and sensor accuracy.

Field safety

Follow the manufacturer IOM/service manual and site LOTO procedures. Never bypass a safety to keep a chiller running. Confirm pressure-temperature data for the actual refrigerant before making charge decisions.
